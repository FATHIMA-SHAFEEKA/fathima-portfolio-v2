01 / Project overview

Good food deserves a better destination.

FoodLoop explores how donors and receivers could coordinate surplus-food redistribution with less confusion, more trust and clearer pickup arrangements.

The opportunity

Surplus food is time-sensitive. A donor may be ready to give food away, but finding someone who can collect it at the right time can be difficult.

FoodLoop focuses on the complete experience: posting food, browsing availability, claiming, arranging collection and confirming handover.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

How can donors and receivers coordinate time-sensitive food sharing more reliably?

The concept prioritises quick posting, visible availability, pickup windows, food-safety information and handover confirmation.

This problem framing is based on secondary research and unvalidated stakeholder assumptions.

02 / UX research

Understanding the problem space.

I began with secondary research and hypothetical stakeholder scenarios to explore potential barriers to food donation and collection.

Research transparency

This was assumption-based proto-research conducted in October 2026. No real participants were interviewed. AI-assisted scenario answers and illustrative quotes are hypotheses, not real interview findings.

Research method

I created four hypothetical stakeholder profiles and drafted possible answers to eight interview questions, informed by secondary-source context.

Secondary sources

SharePlate research paper, Robin Hood Army Sri Lanka, and articles about food waste and redistribution in Sri Lanka.

Stakeholders considered

  • Small neighbourhood bakery owner
  • Hotel or event caterer
  • Charity volunteer / receiver
  • Household donor

Research focus

  • Surplus-food handling
  • Barriers to donating
  • Collection coordination
  • Trust and food-safety concerns
  • Preferred devices and workflows

Three illustrative personas helped connect stakeholder assumptions to possible product requirements.

Illustrative personas

Nadeesha, Kasun and Shalini are fictional profiles. Their ages, names and quotes are illustrative. The hotel/caterer was considered in the research but is not presented as a separate detailed persona.

Donor · Bakery owner

“I would donate more if someone reliably came to collect it.”

Receiver · Student volunteer

04 / Research synthesis

From assumptions to design priorities.

Eight potential themes were identified from the hypothetical scenarios. They remain hypotheses until tested with real stakeholders.

01

Pickup is the bottleneck

Fixed pickup windows, claim confirmation, pickup code and no-show handling.

02

Coordination gets buried

One live listing feed with visible status and alerts.

03

Fear of unsafe food

Safety acknowledgements and clear food-safety information.

04

Trust needs proof

Food photos, verification indicators, contact options and donation records.

05

Timing matters

Fast posting, visible pickup times and urgent listing states.

06

Phone-first behaviour

Mobile-first layouts with short forms and clear actions.

07

Managers need oversight

Potential future desktop dashboard and donation records.

08

Small donors need a route

Explore drop-off points as a future feature.

Three working hypotheses

HYPOTHESIS 01

Predictable pickup times may encourage regular donations.

HYPOTHESIS 02

Receivers may need earlier notice of quantity and pickup time.

HYPOTHESIS 03

Both sides may need visible safety and trust information.

06 / Information architecture & user flows

Designing the complete journey.

The MVP focuses on posting, browsing, claiming, collecting and confirming food handover.

01

Sitemap / Information architecture

Four page groups: public pages, account and system pages, donor pages and receiver pages.

Public pages Account / system Donor area Receiver area
MVP: Post, browse, claim, collect and impact. About, History and extended Profile functionality are planned for later.
02

Donor user flow

A streamlined journey for posting surplus food and completing a confirmed pickup.

Sign up→ Post food→ Publish→ Receiver claims→ Pickup code→ Complete
Key design decision: New donors can move directly from sign-up to posting food, avoiding an unnecessary dashboard step. Returning donors can use their dashboard.
Alternative states: No connection while posting, expired listing, donor cancellation and missed pickup.
03

Receiver user flow

A journey for discovering available food, claiming safely and completing collection.

Sign up / area→ Browse→ Open listing→ Safety & claim→ Pickup code→ Collect
Key design decisions: Show listing status, provide a Call Donor button and propose a reminder 30 minutes before the pickup window ends.

Edge cases considered

The experience must remain understandable when the ideal journey cannot be completed.

09 / Reflection & next steps

What comes next?

What this process taught me

Food redistribution is not only a discovery problem. The experience also needs to consider timing, coordination, safety and confirmation between two different user groups.

Creating the flows before the final UI helped identify alternative states that a simple happy-path design could miss.

What I would validate next

  • Interview 3–5 real bakery owners and charity volunteers.
  • Test acceptance of fixed pickup windows.
  • Check whether receivers understand pickup codes.
  • Evaluate the final prototype with real users.
  • Revise assumptions that are not supported by evidence.
